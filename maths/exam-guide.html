📄 Exam Technique Guide

✅ Before-Exam Checklist
🔖 Maths Command Words

These words tell you exactly what the examiner wants. Knowing them earns marks!

Calculate
Work out the numerical answer. Show all working steps.
e.g. “Calculate the area of the circle.”
Show that
Prove the given result using full mathematical working. The answer is given — you need to get there.
e.g. “Show that x = 4.”
Estimate
Round to 1 significant figure first, then calculate. Don't give an exact answer!
e.g. “Estimate 48.3 × 19.7” → 50 × 20 = 1000
Simplify
Write in the simplest form (collect like terms, cancel fractions, simplify ratio).
e.g. “Simplify 3x + 5x − x” → 7x
Solve
Find the value of the unknown. Always check your answer by substituting back.
e.g. “Solve 3x + 2 = 14” → x = 4
Expand
Multiply out the brackets and collect like terms.
e.g. “Expand 3(2x − 5)” → 6x − 15
Factorise
Write as a product using brackets. Take out the HCF or factorise the quadratic.
e.g. “Factorise x² + 5x + 6” → (x+2)(x+3)
Prove
Demonstrate using algebra or geometry that something is always true. Use general variables.
e.g. “Prove that the sum of two consecutive integers is odd.”
Write down / State
No working needed — just give the answer directly.
e.g. “Write down the gradient of y = 3x + 1” → 3
Describe
State all key features in words. For transformations: type, direction, amount, centre.
e.g. “Describe fully the transformation.”
Sketch / Draw
Sketch: approximate shape with key features labelled. Draw: accurate diagram using instruments.
e.g. “Sketch y = x² − 4”
Give a reason / Explain
State the mathematical rule or theorem (e.g. “alternate angles are equal”).
e.g. “Give a reason for your answer.”
💡 Top 10 Exam Tips
1

Always show your working

Even a wrong final answer can earn method marks if your working is clear. Write every step.

2

Check with the inverse

Finished an equation? Substitute your answer back in. After multiplication, divide to check.

3

Use estimation to sense-check

Round to 1 sig fig before calculating. If your answer is wildly different, you've made an error.

4

Draw diagrams for geometry

Always sketch the situation for geometry, bearings, and word problems. Label everything.

5

Check your units throughout

Area in cm², volume in cm³. Convert to consistent units before calculating speed, density, etc.

6

Read the question twice

Underline what you're being asked to find. So many marks are lost by answering a different question.

7

Don't round too early

Keep full decimal values until the final answer. Only round at the end, to the precision asked.

8

Look at the marks available

A 4-mark question needs 4 steps of working. A 1-mark question just needs the answer.

9

For “Show that” questions

Work from one side to reach the given answer. Never start with the answer — work towards it.

10

Attempt every question

Even if you're unsure, write down a relevant formula or method. You might earn a mark.

🌟 Mark Scheme Practice

Choose which student working deserves full marks. Think like an examiner!

📄 Question: Solve 3x + 5 = 20. [2 marks]
Which student gets full marks?
Student A: “x = 5”
Student B: “3x = 20 − 5 = 15, so x = 5”
Student C: “3x = 25, x = 8.3”
📄 Question: A triangle has base 8cm and perpendicular height 6cm. Find the area. [2 marks]
Which student gets full marks?
Student A: “Area = 8 × 6 = 48 cm²”
Student B: “Area = ½ × 8 × 6 = 24 cm²”
Student C: “A = ½ × 8 + 6 = 10 cm²”
📄 Question: Give a reason why angle ABC = angle DEF. [1 mark]
Which answer gets the mark?
Student A: “Because they look the same size”
Student B: “Alternate angles are equal (Z-angles between parallel lines)”
Student C: “Corresponding angles (F-angles)”
📄 Question: A sale reduces a price by 30%. The sale price is £56. Find the original price. [3 marks]
Which student gets full marks?
Student A: “56 + 30% = 56 + 16.80 = £72.80”
Student B: “Multiplier = 0.7, original = 56 × 0.7 = £39.20”
Student C: “70% = £56, so 1% = £0.80, original = 100% = £80”

⚠ Common Mistakes to Avoid
Forgetting the ½ in triangle area ⌄

Area of triangle = ½ × base × height, NOT base × height. The ½ is always required.

Using diameter instead of radius in circle formulas ⌄

A = πr² needs the RADIUS. If given diameter, always halve it first: r = d ÷ 2.

Rounding too early in multi-step calculations ⌄

Keep full accuracy throughout and only round at the final step. Premature rounding causes errors.

Not converting time to decimal hours for SDT ⌄

2 hours 30 minutes = 2.5 hours (not 2.30!). Minutes ÷ 60 to get decimal hours.

Mixing up percentage increase/decrease and reverse percentage ⌄

To increase by 15%: multiply by 1.15. To reverse a 15% reduction: divide by 0.85 (NOT multiply by 1.15 — that's a common error!).

Bearings: not using 3 digits or measuring from wrong direction ⌄

Bearings are always measured clockwise from NORTH and written with 3 digits. 45° = 045°, not 45°.
